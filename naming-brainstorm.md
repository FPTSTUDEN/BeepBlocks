## User

> im thinking some name with computer-ish vibe like beep bop

## Kiro

Here are some names in that beep-boop direction, grouped by feel:

**Robot noises**
- **Bleep** – short and playful. "Bleep scripts" sounds natural.
- **Beepbox** – a box of beeps: blocks plus a sandbox.
- **Whirr** – the sound of a machine working through your files.
- **Clunk** – friendly and a bit clumsy, a nice fit for beginners.

**Little helper bot**
- **Botbench** – a workbench where you build little bots.
- **Tinkerbot** – you tinker, and it does the chores.
- **Chorebot** – says exactly what it does: automates boring file jobs.

**Bits and blocks**
- **Bitblocks** – direct, says "blocks" on the label.
- **Bytesize** – small programs, with a pun.
- **Blip** – one step at a time, like the step-through timeline.

My pick is **Bleep**. It's one syllable, easy to say and spell, and it reads well everywhere it would show up:
- the window title: "Bleep"
- the file extension: `.bleep.json`
- the exported script header: "Generated by Bleep"
- the stderr line: `Bleep: Error at block …`

Runner-up is **Tinkerbot**, if you want the "it does chores for you" angle more than the sound effect.

Before you commit, it's worth checking that the name isn't already taken by a similar tool; short names like Blip and Bleep tend to be. I can do a quick web search on your top 2–3 picks.

Once you choose, the rename is about 15 minutes:
- the UI title and `index.html`
- the project format string, project file extension and localStorage keys (no saved projects exist outside your browser yet, so nothing breaks)
- the script header
- the spec titles

The IR's internal format string (`block-playground-ir`) can stay as it is or be renamed too; your call.

## Kiro

Here are some different directions, each with a different idea behind it:

**Workshop and craft** (you build tools that do real jobs)
- **Jig** – a woodworker's jig is a homemade guide for doing a repetitive job exactly the same way every time. That's what an exported script is.
- **Whittle** – you shape a small program bit by bit.
- **Toolshed** – where your homemade tools live.

**Recipes and kitchens** (step by step, then run it for real)
- **Recipe** – a program as a list of steps. Maybe too generic.
- **Mise** – from *mise en place*: get everything ready, try it out, then cook. That mirrors preview, then `--apply`.

**Rehearsal and stage** (practice safely, then do it for real)
- **Dry Run** – the core safety feature, used as the name.
- **Rehearse** – run it on the sandbox first, then perform it on real files.
- **Stagehand** – does the heavy lifting behind the scenes.

**Spells and magic** (a little incantation that does something real)
- **Cantrip** – in D&D, a small, simple spell you can cast again and again. That's a good metaphor for a short beginner script.
- **Spellbook** – your saved projects.

**Paths and maps** (the file explorer and step-through)
- **Trailhead** – where beginners start.
- **Breadcrumb** – follow each step, and click to trace back to the block.

**Plain and friendly**
- **Tidy** – most starter tasks are cleanup: sorting downloads, renaming photos. It's narrower if you add domains later, like the text document theme.
- **Errand** – a small job you send something off to do.

My picks from these:
1. **Jig** – short and unusual, with a real meaning that fits "build once, run on real files". It's the most distinctive name here.
2. **Cantrip** – memorable and fun, and fits the beginner-friendly tone. It's a bit niche if your users don't play games.
3. **Rehearse** – says exactly what the app is for: practice safely, then do it for real.

## User

> what about more stylized like n8n

## Kiro

n8n is a numeronym: n + 8 letters + n, short for "nodemation". Here are stylized names in that spirit, including a few other styles:

**Numeronyms** (first letter, count, last letter)
- **b3p** – "beep", stylized. Reads like a tiny CLI tool.
- **s5x** – "sandbox". Hard to guess, but looks cool.
- **a8n** – "automation". Too close to n8n, which is also an automation tool, so probably avoid.
- **p9d** – "playground". Clean, but nobody will know how to say it.

**Letters and numbers as sounds**
- **b33p** – leetspeak beep. Playful, and keeps your first instinct.
- **bl0ks** – blocks with a zero. Easy to read.
- **scr1pt** – too generic.
- **2do** – "to-do": small jobs to get done.

**Lowercase compound words** (like esbuild or zx)
- **blokz** – blocks with a z. Fun, a bit 2000s.
- **runbox** – run it in the box first.
- **dryrn** – dry run, squashed.
- **fyle** – files, stylized. Tied to the file domain, though.

**Symbol-ish**
- **b.op** – beep/bop with a dot. Looks like a file extension or a method call.
- **beep()** – a function call as a name. Very "computer".
- **~bop** – the tilde makes it look like a home-folder thing. Fun, but awkward to type in some places.

My picks:
1. **b33p** – keeps the beep-boop vibe you started with, and it's instantly readable and pronounceable. It works in lowercase everywhere: `.b33p.json`, `Generated by b33p`.
2. **beep()** – the most "computer" of the lot. In practice you'd write it as **beep** in file names and keep the `()` only in the logo.
3. **b3p** – the closest to n8n's style while still being guessable.

One catch with this style: people need to know how to say it. n8n gets away with it because it's well known. For a beginner tool, a name people can read aloud on first sight (like b33p) usually works better than a pure numeronym.

## User

> what about names that seem unrelated but still have the vibe of the software's functionality like homebrew scoop choco

## Kiro

Those names work because each is an everyday word that hints at what the tool does: homebrew is "make it yourself at home", scoop is "grab what you need", and choco is a sweet treat. Here are names built the same way, from ideas behind this app:

**Step-by-step motion** (the timeline you can scrub through)
- **Marble**: like a marble run. You build the track from pieces (blocks), drop the marble in, and watch it roll through step by step. This is the closest match to what the app actually does.
- **Domino**: set up a chain and watch it go.
- **Abacus**: computing by hand, one bead (step) at a time. Old-school, but it fits.

**Sorting and compartments** (the file chores)
- **Bento**: neat compartments, like files sorted into folders by type.
- **Tacklebox**: everything in its little slot.
- **Pantry**: shelves, labels, everything put away.
- **Broom**: sweeps a messy folder clean.
- **Sift**: shake the folder and keep only what matches (the find-and-act block).

**Safe play first** (the sandbox, then running for real)
- **Sandcastle**: build it in the sandbox before the real thing.
- **Puddle**: splash around safely before going in the sea.
- **Chalk**: sketch it out, rub it out, draw it again. That's what the preview is.

**Kitchen** (homebrew's own family)
- **Whisk**: quick little jobs.
- **Kettle**: put it on and it does its thing.
- **Ladle**: scoop's cousin.

**Folding** (because folders)
- **Origami**: folding step by step, and you can follow each fold.

My picks:
1. **Marble**: the marble-run metaphor explains the app by itself. You build a track from blocks and watch the marble go through it, and the playground could even show the current step as a little marble.
2. **Bento**: friendly, short, and the "everything in its compartment" image fits the sort and rename tasks.
3. **Sandcastle**: it captures the core promise of building safely in a sandbox, then doing it for real.

Short everyday words like these tend to be taken somewhere already; for example, Bento is used by other software products. Tell me your top two or three and I'll check whether they collide with existing developer or education tools before you settle.
